Matching the stimulus to the nerve: a narrative review of selective surface electrical stimulation of the facial muscles in peripheral facial palsy

Background. Electrical stimulation (ES) of the facial muscles has been used in peripheral facial palsy for decades, yet guidelines do not recommend it and published protocols vary widely. Much of the disagreement may reflect how stimulation was applied rather than whether it can work.

Objective. To summarise the evidence on the effectiveness and safety of facial ES and to derive, from the same evidence, the principles and starting parameters of a selective, nerve-status-matched approach.

Methods. Narrative review following the SANRA criteria. Guidelines, systematic reviews, clinical, cohort, laboratory and anatomical studies, textbooks and regulatory documents were identified from database searches and citation tracking, appraised for design, quality and relevance, and synthesised narratively; the authors' inferences are labelled.

Results. A 2026 meta-analysis found moderate-certainty evidence that add-on ES started within seven days reduces incomplete recovery in Bell's palsy (risk ratio 0.65, 95% CI 0.48–0.88), but 13 of its 14 trials used electroacupuncture. Randomised trials of surface stimulation are few and conflicting, and no guideline recommends ES. Studies have not consistently shown more synkinesis, although a retrospective cohort associated intensive, non-selective acute-phase stimulation with more synkinesis. Innervated muscle responds to short pulses (below 1 ms) through its nerve, whereas denervated muscle needs long pulses (selective responses from 25 ms). We propose two parameter sets chosen for each muscle by the clinician, stimulation of one muscle at a time through a small electrode over its motor point, the lowest intensity that contracts the target muscle only, RMS current density ≤ 2 mA/cm², and a same-side reference away from the face, anterior neck and chest.

Conclusions. Facial ES can be justified only as a selective, supervised adjunct to medical treatment and exercise therapy. The proposed framework is derived, not tested, and needs evaluation in randomised trials that report parameters in full.
